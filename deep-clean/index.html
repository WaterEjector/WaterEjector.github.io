How the Deep Dust Clean Works

Six frequencies, one pass - from deep bass to high treble

Frequency Sweep

Unlike the standard water eject tone, the deep clean cycles through six frequencies from 200Hz up to 1500Hz, spending ten seconds on each. This band is where the speaker diaphragm moves the most, so each step physically shakes debris of a different size loose from the speaker mesh.

When to Use It

Run a deep clean when your speaker sounds muffled or quieter than it used to, when calls sound distant, or after the standard water eject didn't fully restore clarity. Pocket lint and dust build up gradually, so a monthly pass keeps sound crisp.

Getting the Best Result

Set volume to maximum, remove any case, and point the speaker grill downward. For stubborn build-up, run the sweep two or three times back to back - each pass shakes out a little more. Finish with a soft dry brush across the grill if dust is visible.

Keep Every Speaker in Your Life Clean

Maintenance tips for your phone, earbuds and accessories

Earbuds and Bluetooth Speakers Too

Wireless earbuds collect ear wax and pocket lint even faster than phone speakers. Connect your TWS earbuds or Bluetooth speaker to this page and run the same frequency sweep - the tones play through whatever device is connected, shaking dust out of the driver mesh. A monthly pass keeps earbuds sounding new and saves replacing a pair that just needed cleaning.

Prevent Dust Before It Settles

Most speaker dust comes from pockets and bags. A case with raised edges around the speaker cutouts, keeping the phone in a separate pocket from keys and tissues, and an occasional pass with a soft dry brush over the grill all slow the build-up. Avoid compressed air and pins - both push debris deeper into the mesh and can tear the speaker membrane.

When the Speaker Needs Repair

If audio is still low or crackly after several deep cleans, the speaker itself may be worn out - drivers degrade after years of maximum-volume use. Phone speaker replacement at a service centre usually costs ₹800–₹3,000 depending on the model, which is worth weighing against the phone's age. Get a quote before assuming you need a new phone; a speaker swap is one of the cheapest repairs there is.
